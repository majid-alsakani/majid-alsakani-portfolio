Building an AI Job Platform for Yemen

By Majid Al-Sakani (ماجد السكني) · Full Stack Developer, Yemen · Updated 2026-08-03

PythonFastAPIAI/LLMPostgreSQLReact

When I started Joobea, unemployment in Yemen was not a data problem — it was a matching problem. Thousands of capable people and hundreds of real openings existed at the same time, but they never met on the same page. I am Majid Al-Sakani, a full stack developer from Yemen, and this article documents the engineering decisions behind the platform.

1. The constraint shapes the architecture

Most job platforms are designed for fast connections and modern devices. Our users are frequently on 3G, on mid-range Android phones, and paying per megabyte. That single constraint drove every decision: server-rendered critical paths, aggressive payload budgets, image compression at upload time, and a data layer that never sends a field the screen does not render.

2. Smart matching instead of keyword search

Keyword search fails when a candidate writes "محاسب" and the employer writes "Accountant". We normalize both sides into a shared embedding space, then rank candidates with a hybrid score:

  • Semantic similarity between the résumé and the job description.
  • Hard filters for location, contract type and years of experience.
  • Recency and completeness weights, so active and complete profiles surface first.

3. AI CV analysis that gives feedback, not a score

A number tells a job seeker nothing. Our analyzer returns actionable items: missing sections, weak verbs, unquantified achievements, and a rewritten sample line. Prompts are versioned and evaluated against a fixed test set so quality regressions are caught before release.
